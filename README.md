# Assistive Device Design & Materials Research - Humanos 3D

Selected engineering work from my **3D Printing & Assistive Technology internship at Humanos 3D in Medellín, Colombia**.

This repository documents two connected parts of my internship: **human-centered assistive-device design** and **materials research for sustainable additive manufacturing**. My work focused on translating individual user needs into practical 3D-printable devices, iterating geometry and attachment features through prototyping, and evaluating how material choice affects durability, usability, safety, environmental impact, and end-of-life options.

## Featured Work

### Adaptive Knife Holder

The [`design/`](design/) folder contains the CAD and fabrication files for an adaptive knife-holder system developed around the needs of a specific user who required additional stability and control while using a kitchen knife.

The design evolved as a multi-component system rather than a single printed part. The repository includes the primary holder, protective covers, a protection piece, a Velcro-related attachment component, and geometry adapted for right-hand use.

[**View the adaptive knife-holder project ->**](design/)

### Sustainable Materials Research

The [`materials-research/`](materials-research/) folder contains a technical review on material selection for fused filament fabrication (FFF) in custom assistive technology.

The graduate-level portfolio revision develops a decision framework around **PLA, recycled PET-based feedstocks, PHA, process sustainability, print-time emissions, end-of-life pathways, and food-waste-derived composite concepts**. It treats sustainability as a coupled engineering problem involving material properties, print parameters, service life, repairability, user context, and realistic disposal or recycling infrastructure.

[**View the materials research ->**](materials-research/)

## Engineering Approach

Across both projects, I used a common design philosophy: begin with the user's functional need, translate it into engineering requirements, prototype quickly, evaluate fit and usability, and then refine geometry, material, and fabrication decisions around the actual service environment.

The work brought together:

- human-centered and accessibility-focused design;
- Fusion 360 CAD and iterative geometry development;
- FFF/FDM 3D printing and rapid prototyping;
- attachment, fit, comfort, and safety considerations;
- material selection for durability and repeated use;
- life-cycle and end-of-life reasoning for polymer additive manufacturing; and
- technical communication for design and research documentation.

## Broader Internship Work

Additional assistive-device work during the internship included adaptive tools and holders for activities such as exercise, music, writing, and everyday device use. These experiences reinforced the value of rapid prototyping when solutions need to be tailored to an individual rather than optimized for high-volume manufacturing.

## Scope & Responsible Use

This repository is an engineering portfolio documenting design and research work. The CAD files and material-selection guidance are **not medical-device certification, clinical guidance, or a guarantee of safety for any specific user or application**. Load-bearing, food-contact, skin-contact, or otherwise safety-critical use requires device-specific validation, appropriate material/process qualification, and applicable professional or regulatory review.

Client-related media should only be shared publicly when appropriate permission has been confirmed.
